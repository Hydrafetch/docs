Pricing pages move. Competitors quietly add a feature, publish a post or open a role. A supplier changes their terms. You want to know when, without a person checking every week.

A [monitor](/endpoints/monitors) does the watching: it checks your targets on a schedule, works out what changed, and calls your webhook with the change.

## The pipeline

### 1. Pick what to watch

There are three kinds of target, and picking the right one is most of the job.

- **Pages** when you care about what a page says: a pricing page, a changelog, terms of service. You get a diff of the text.
- **A site** when you care about what gets published: a blog, a careers section, a product catalogue. You get each new page with its title and opening lines, and pages that go away.
- **A search** when you care about what others say: coverage, mentions, reviews. You get results that were never there before.

A handful of pages you chose produces signal. Watching everything produces a feed nobody reads.

### 2. Create the monitor

```bash
curl -X POST https://api.hydrafetch.com/v1/web/monitors \
  -H "X-API-Key: $HYDRAFETCH_API_KEY" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{
    "name": "Rival watch",
    "schedule": { "every": "1d" },
    "targets": [
      { "type": "pages", "urls": ["https://rival.io/pricing", "https://rival.io/terms"] },
      { "type": "site", "url": "https://rival.io/blog", "includePaths": ["^/blog/.+"] }
    ],
    "webhook": { "url": "https://your-app.com/hooks/hydrafetch", "secret": "'"$WEBHOOK_SECRET"'" }
  }'
```

The first check runs within a minute and records how everything looks today. From then on, each check reports what changed since the last one.

### 3. Receive the change

Each change arrives as a `monitor.change` call. Verify the signature first, as described in [Verifying a webhook](/concepts/jobs-and-webhooks#verifying-a-webhook), then use the `status`:

- `changed`: show the `diff`, and `fieldChanges` when a price or availability moved.
- `new`: show the `title` and `excerpt`, with a link to the `url`.
- `removed`: the page was missing from two checks in a row.
- `error`: the page could not be read three checks in a row.

`monitor.check.completed` arrives once a check ends, with its counts. Use it for a summary, or to notice a watch that went quiet.

## Making the alert worth reading

An alert saying "rival.io/pricing changed" is a task, not information. Every change already carries the diff or the new page's opening lines, so put that in the message.

Then cut what you do not care about, before it reaches anyone.

- **A goal** in plain words, like `a plan gets cheaper` or `senior roles in Berlin`, alerts only on changes that match it and keeps the rest in the history with the reason it was held back. 1 credit per change checked.
- **`selector`** compares only part of a page, such as `#pricing`, so a new footer link or a rotating testimonial never fires.
- **`triggerOn`** alerts only when a changed line contains a word you name, like `price` or `/sold out/i`.
- **`ignore`** leaves out lines that change without meaning anything.

Relative times, clock times, view counts and tracking parameters are ignored already, so a page that only shows a newer "updated 5 minutes ago" counts as unchanged.

## Knowing the watch still works

A watch that fails silently is worse than none, because you believe you are covered.

Monitors report their own health. A page that cannot be read three checks in a row sends an `error` alert instead of staying quiet, and a monitor whose checks fail ten times in a row pauses itself with `pausedReason: "failing"` and says so in its last `monitor.check.completed` call. Every check, including the quiet ones, is in `GET /v1/web/monitors/{id}/checks`, so a month without changes can be told apart from a month without checks.

A site target lists the site again on every check, so a page at a URL you never knew existed still gets noticed.

## What it costs

- Six pages checked daily: 6 credits a day, about 180 a month.
- A blog checked daily: 1 credit a day, plus 1 for each new post read.
- A goal adds 1 credit for each change it checks, not for every check, so on a quiet page it costs almost nothing.

Pages that cannot be read are free.

## What to watch for

Daily is almost always enough. Nobody's pricing page changes hourly, and the shortest schedules are for status pages and stock, not for terms of service. A monitor with a search target checks at most once an hour.

Start without a goal for a week, read what the monitor reports, then add a goal or a `selector` for the noise you actually saw. Guessing the noise in advance usually filters out the change you wanted.
