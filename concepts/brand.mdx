Give the API a domain and get back the company in a form you can display. Not a favicon and a guessed colour: logos marked with the background they work on, colours ranked by how much the site uses them, fonts and social accounts.

## Why a favicon is not a logo

To show another company inside your product you need a mark that works on a dark background, a colour that is really theirs, and the right name. A favicon and the first colour in a stylesheet give you none of that.

- Favicons are 32 pixels square, and often just a letter in a box.
- The first colour in a stylesheet is usually a border, not the brand.
- Nothing in a page's markup says which logo was drawn for a dark background.

## Look up a domain

```bash
curl "https://api.hydrafetch.com/v1/web/brand?domain=stripe.com" \
  -H "X-API-Key: hf_your_key_here"
```

- To look up many domains in one call, `POST` a list to the same path.
- To find a company by name, use `GET /v1/web/brand/search?query=stripe`. It searches brands we already hold, returns the domain, name, main logo and main colour for each, and is free.
- To get just one logo, use [Logo](/endpoints/logo).

## What comes back

<ResponseField name="name, description, tagline" type="string">
  How the company describes itself. Also `legalName` and a contact `email` when the site publishes them.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="assets" type="array">
  Logos and images. Each has a `kind` (`wordmark`, `symbol`, `favicon` or `backdrop`), a `variant` (`light`, `dark` or `mono`) saying which background it works on, its `format` and `resolution`, the colours inside it, and a `blurhash` you can show as a placeholder while the image loads.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="colors" type="array">
  The palette. Each colour has a `hex`, the nearest CSS colour `name`, a `weight` for how much the site uses it, and sometimes a `role`: `primary`, `accent`, `background` or `text`. The weight is what tells you which one is the brand colour.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="fonts" type="array">
  Font families with their `weight`, and a `role` of `heading` or `body` where we can tell.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="socials" type="array">
  Each account's `platform`, `url` and `handle` without the `@`, so you can match it against other data you hold.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="links" type="object">
  The company's own pages when we find them: `pricing`, `blog`, `careers`, `contact`, `privacy`, `terms` and `docs`.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="naics" type="object">
  The company's industry as a NAICS code and title, with a `confidence`.
</ResponseField>

## Where each field came from

The `sources` object says, for each field, where its value came from. "The site says this" and "a model guessed this" are different claims, and they should not look the same in your database.

| Source | Means | Trust |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `declared` | The site said it, in JSON-LD, OpenGraph or microdata | Highest |
| `harvested` | We processed the actual image, or read it from the site's own CSS | High |
| `inferred` | A model worked it out from the page | Useful, but never presented as fact |
| `external` | Another data source, not the site itself | Depends on the source |

A field can also have a `confidence`. Together they let you choose a fallback instead of showing something wrong: a low-confidence colour can give way to your own default, while a `declared` logo is safe to use.

## Design systems

`GET /v1/web/styleguide` returns the design behind the brand: how the site combines its colours, type, spacing and components.

```bash
curl "https://api.hydrafetch.com/v1/web/styleguide?domain=stripe.com" \
  -H "X-API-Key: hf_your_key_here"
```

The design also comes back ready to paste:

- `css` is a stylesheet with an `@font-face` rule for each web font and a `:root` block of CSS variables for colours, fonts, the type scale, corner radius, shadows and spacing.
- `tailwind` holds the same variables as a Tailwind CSS v4 theme. After pasting it, classes such as `bg-accent`, `font-heading`, `text-h1`, `rounded-button` and `shadow-md` use the site's design.
- `shadcn` carries the colours, radius and fonts under shadcn/ui variable names, ready to replace the `:root` block in a shadcn project's `globals.css`.
- `dark` lists the site's dark-theme colours when it has one, and every export then gains a matching dark block. When the site has no dark theme, `dark` is `null`.

Only plain colours, lengths, font names and https font files are included, so nothing else from the page can reach your stylesheet.

Tokens alone rarely make a site recognisable, so `components` describes how its parts are drawn: the border, corners, padding and shadow most panels share, the top navigation bar, tabs, any full-width band in a contrasting colour, and where panel titles sit. `button` and `secondaryButton` cover the two main calls to action. Each comes with a `css` string you can paste into a rule as it is. To match a site closely, look at a [screenshot](/endpoints/media) of the page next to its styleguide.

Pass `url` instead of `domain` to read one page, such as a pricing page styled differently from the homepage. A domain's styleguide is kept for 30 days and a single page's for 7. Pass `maxAge` in milliseconds to accept only a newer result, or `0` to read the page fresh.
